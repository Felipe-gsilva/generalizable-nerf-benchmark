\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) have consolidated themselves in recent years as the state-of-the-art for novel view synthesis, in both industry and academia \cite{kerbl3Dgaussians, Tancik2023, substance3dviewer}. However, the most advanced neural rendering techniques demand vast amounts of: (i) computational power; and (ii) multiple views in the training dataset. Both challenges make practical applications—such as mobile scanning, virtual try-ons, and synthesis in reduced datasets—unfeasible. To address this problem, projects such as Instant-NGP (\textit{Instant Neural Graphics Primitives}), Nerfacto, PixelNeRF, and GNT (\textit{Generalizable NeRF Transformer}) \cite{muller2022, Tancik2023, yu2021pixelnerfneuralradiancefields, t2023attentionnerfneeds}, among others, attempt to expand the state-of-the-art and, consequently, tackle analogous themes, such as: (i) spatial parameter \textit{encoding} \cite{muller2022}; (ii) the use of hybrid architectures combining Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViT) \cite{schwarz2021grafgenerativeradiancefields, wu2021cvt, t2023attentionnerfneeds}; and (iii) the pre-computation (\textit{baking}) of models into structures with a smaller memory footprint \cite{hedman2021bakingneuralradiancefields}.

Consequently, this work aims to explore the use of different neural radiance field models and their respective optimization techniques by applying them to sparse-view datasets, promoting an exploratory analysis between computational performance and the visual quality of the generated volumes. To evaluate reconstruction fidelity, visual quality metrics widely established in the literature will be used, such as PSNR (\textit{Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio}), SSIM (\textit{Structural Similarity Index Measure}) \cite{wang2004image}, and LPIPS (\textit{Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity}) \cite{zhang2018unreasonable}. In parallel, the quantification of operational costs will be based on systemic performance metrics, including convergence time during training, inference speed, and memory \textit{footprint}, especially VRAM (\textit{Video Random Access Memory}) consumption. Although works like \cite{niemeyer2022regnerf} and \cite{kim2023poserefinement} have evaluated NeRFs in limited data regimes, and projects like NerfBaselines \cite{kulhanek2025nerfbaselinesconsistentreproducibleevaluation} have reported results on traditional and per-scene training-based NeRFs, a systematic comparison between generalizable models with convolutional extractors versus attention-based ones, under controlled hardware conditions and reproducible splits, remains unexplored in the literature. In short, this investigation provides a consolidated panorama of the \textit{trade-offs} between computational efficiency and visual fidelity, establishing clear guidelines for the viability and practical application of novel view synthesis models in highly constrained scenarios, particularly regarding data and hardware limitations.

Given this problem, the contributions of this work are: (i) a systematic and reproducible evaluation of few-shot and per-scene NeRF models under three data regimes; and (ii) the public release of integration wrappers for PixelNeRF and GNT into the Nerfstudio framework at \url{https://github.com/felipe-gsilva/few-shot-nerfs}, adapting their input and output interfaces to comply with the framework's unified evaluation protocol, without modifying the pre-trained weights or original inference logic, to allow reproducible comparisons under controlled conditions.
