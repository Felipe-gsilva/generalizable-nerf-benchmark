\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}

This section presents the consolidated quantitative, computational and qualitative analyses across the evaluated LLFF scenes. Table \ref{tab:super_results} summarizes the global averages for visual fidelity, temporal efficiency, and memory footprint. The results reveal critical trade-offs between generalization, optimization stability, and hardware constraints.

\subsection{Visual Fidelity}
The per-scene architectures demonstrated vastly superior visual reconstruction capabilities. Nerfacto established the performance upper bound, achieving a global average of 27.11 dB PSNR and 0.875 SSIM (Table \ref{tab:super_results}). In contrast, the generalizable models struggled significantly with the sparse constraints of the LLFF dataset.

PixelNeRF failed to converge on any LLFF scene (§II-A), a behavior attributable to its reliance on dense multi-view spatial coverage for accurate 2D-to-3D feature projection. The purely local 2D feature projection via the frozen CNN encoder failed to triangulate depth accurately under extreme sparsity, resulting in severe isotropic blurring and a complete failure to reconstruct the underlying geometry. GNT, conversely, achieved structurally superior reconstructions relative to PixelNeRF within the same constrained optimization budget. Although not able to converge to the final scene's minor details, the PSNR values ranged from 11.58 to 12.96, as observed in Table \ref{tab:room_n_views}. This demonstrates that GNT, as complex as it is and despite its more robust cross-attention volumetric aggregation, still faces significant representation bottlenecks under strict few-shot regimes. The inability to fully capture high-frequency, in-the-wild textures highlights the limitations of keeping the feature extraction network frozen during adaptation, forcing the model to rely on generalized priors that ultimately struggle to resolve out-of-distribution geometric complexities.

Furthermore, the stability of these visual results across different scenes is illustrated in Fig. \ref{fig:boxplot}. The boxplot highlights the significant performance variance inherent to each paradigm. Generalizable models (PixelNeRF and GNT) exhibit a highly constrained PSNR distribution, reflecting a consistent inability to capture high-frequency details regardless of the scene geometry. In contrast, per-scene models, particularly Nerfacto, demonstrate not only a significantly higher median PSNR but also a wider variance. This wider spread indicates that their reconstruction fidelity is highly sensitive to the intrinsic complexity of the specific scene being optimized, yet their worst-case performance still comfortably exceeds the upper bounds of the generalizable baselines.

\subsection{Computational Trade-offs}
From a systemic viability perspective, a clear dichotomy emerges between inference latency and training overhead. While generalizable models bypass extensive optimization (requiring only seconds for Zero-Shot inference or $\sim$7 minutes for TTA), their volumetric feature aggregation mechanisms result in catastrophic inference bottlenecks, yielding rendering speeds below 0.02 FPS. 

Conversely, the explicit multiscale hash representations of the per-scene models proved vastly more efficient during runtime. Despite requiring lengthier convergence periods (from $\sim$9 minutes for Nerfacto up to $\sim$32 minutes for Instant-NGP), they achieve inference rates up to $97\times$ faster than the generalizable baseline (e.g., Nerfacto at 0.388 FPS vs. PixelNeRF at 0.004 FPS). Furthermore, the hash-based models exhibit a highly optimized dynamic memory footprint, with Instant-NGP operating at just 1.09 GB of VRAM during inference, solidifying per-scene methods as the most viable path for hardware-constrained deployments despite the initial training cost.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis}

The qualitative assessment visually corroborates the quantitative metrics, specifically illustrating the boundaries of each architectural paradigm. For visual clarity, we synthesized novel views matching the exact poses from the test set using perspective projection.

Fig. \ref{fig:comparacao_qualitativa} highlights the severe disparities in reconstruction quality on the highly detailed \textit{fern} scene. As visually evident, neither PixelNeRF nor GNT achieved proper convergence. PixelNeRF produced renderings with intense isotropic blurring, a typical artifact indicating the network's failure to accurately localize 2D projected features across extremely sparse views. GNT, on the other hand, demonstrated a complete generalization failure regarding the \textit{in-the-wild} characteristics of the scene. This resulted in a representational visual collapse, heavily distorting the volumetric geometry into noise—which persisted even after the 10-view Test-Time Adaptation (TTA). 

Conversely, the per-scene architectures successfully captured the intricate foliage geometry. While Instant-NGP exhibited minor volumetric density fluctuations (floaters) in sparsely covered peripheral regions, Nerfacto achieved the highest perceptual fidelity across all scenes (PSNR 27.11 dB, SSIM 0.875, LPIPS 0.171), recovering fine-grained foliage details consistent with the ground truth resolution (Fig. \ref{fig:comparacao_qualitativa}, bottom right).
