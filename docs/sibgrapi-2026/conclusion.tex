\section{Conclusion}
In this paper, we explored the pros and cons of the usage of Per-Scene and Few-Shot NeRFs in order to guide the selection of 3D rendering models in real-world and scientific applications with hardware and data scarcity constraints. It was observed that per-scene architectures are the unequivocal choice whenever inference latency and VRAM are the binding constraints, given their order-of-magnitude advantage in both axes. However, this conclusion does not generalize to acquisition-constrained domains: per-scene models require dense multi-view capture that is often physically infeasible (e.g., histology, holotomography), a scenario where generalizable models remain the only viable option despite their current fidelity ceiling.

While this study provides a consolidated analysis of novel view synthesis under sparse constraints, we acknowledge certain limitations. The strict hardware constraints of our isolated environment (6GB VRAM) precluded the evaluation of architectures that rely on heavy 3D cost volumes or extensive geometric regularizations, such as RegNeRF. Furthermore, our empirical validation was restricted to the forward-facing LLFF dataset. Future work includes expanding this evaluation protocol to unbounded, 360-degree datasets to verify these architectural trade-offs under complex geometries. Additionally, we intend to investigate the integration of diffusion models as generative priors to mitigate the representational collapse observed in generalizable models during few-shot adaptation.
