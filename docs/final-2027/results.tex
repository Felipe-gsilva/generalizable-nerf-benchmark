\subsection{Visual Fidelity}
The per-scene architectures demonstrated vastly superior visual reconstruction capabilities under dense optimization. Nerfacto established the performance upper bound, achieving a global average of 27.11 dB PSNR and 0.875 SSIM (Table \ref{tab:super_results}). In contrast, the generalizable models exhibited marked differences in their capacity to exploit structural priors versus fine-tuning under sparse view constraints ($N \le 10$).

In purely zero-shot (ZS) inference, PixelNeRF produced degenerate reconstructions across all LLFF scenes (6.35 dB global average, Table \ref{tab:mixed_model_comparison}), since its structural priors were insufficient for accurate depth triangulation under sparsity. In contrast, GNT achieved better performative zero-shot reconstructions (10.51 dB, Table \ref{tab:mixed_model_comparison}) leveraging its cross-attention transformer to aggregate global geometric priors even though the images were not scructurally representative.

When fine-tuned on the sparse novel views, both generalizable models demonstrated substantial fidelity gains under appropriate parameter-efficient strategies, overcoming the premature representational collapse observed under naive full fine-tuning. As shown in Table \ref{tab:super_results}, GNT reached 15.07 dB PSNR (and up to 17.46 dB on structured scenes such as \textit{fortress}), surpassing Instant-NGP's sparse-view baseline (14.06 dB). Similarly, PixelNeRF recovered from near-total failure to 10.92 dB PSNR under backbone-specific fine-tuning (Table \ref{tab:mixed_model_comparison}).

Furthermore, the stability of these visual results across different scenes is illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:boxplot}. The boxplot highlights the significant performance variance inherent to each paradigm. While per-scene models exhibit sensitivity to geometric scene complexity (with Instant-NGP ranging from 19.33 dB down to 13.51 dB depending on texture), generalizable models exhibit tight inter-scene variance in zero-shot mode that expands upon fine-tuning as models adapt to high-frequency scene details.

The empirical evaluation reveals a clear architectural divergence in how generalizable backbones balance adaptation capacity and geometric stability during sparse-view fine-tuning.

For PixelNeRF, performance peaks when fine-tuning is restricted to the feature extraction backbone (\textit{PFT-Backbone}, reaching $10.92 \pm 0.26$ dB) or to the additive bias parameters (\textit{BitFit}, $10.79 \pm 0.19$ dB), substantially surpassing unconstrained \textit{Full-FT} ($9.03 \pm 0.11$ dB). Because PixelNeRF projects 2D features along camera rays, its primary bottleneck resides in extracting view-consistent representations from wide-baseline sparse cameras. Updating the ResNet backbone adjusts local feature maps to out-of-distribution textures and sensor profiles, whereas updating all weights simultaneously destabilizes the downstream MLP under sparse supervision. Moreover, PixelNeRF exhibits acute sensitivity to optimization rates, requiring conservative updates ($\eta = 10^{-4}$) to prevent catastrophic geometric divergence.

In contrast, GNT achieves its highest reconstruction fidelity under \textit{PFT-Decoder} ($15.07 \pm 0.03$ dB PSNR, $0.679$ LPIPS), statistically matching \textit{Full-FT} ($15.06 \pm 0.03$ dB) while eliminating visual transformer optimizer memory overhead and reducing per-scene fine-tuning latency from 357.1 s down to 298.8 s (a $16.3\%$ speedup). Because GNT already possesses robust global visual representations from pre-training, freezing its Vision Transformer backbone prevents representation drift and directs gradient descent exclusively into the epipolar cross-attention layers to resolve scene-specific radiance integration. In stark contrast, \textit{PFT-Backbone} yields $10.51 \pm 0.11$ dB—virtually indistinguishable from zero-shot inference—confirming that GNT's domain adaptation is governed almost entirely by its epipolar transformer rather than low-level patch projections.

Consistent with observations reported across foundation models in other modalities \cite{gupta2024beyondlora}, selective and low-rank PEFT demonstrate remarkable stability under sparse supervision. In GNT, \textit{BitFit} reaches $13.12 \pm 0.05$ dB (+2.61 dB over zero-shot) by updating solely additive bias vectors (fewer than $0.5\%$ of parameters), while \textit{PEFT-LoRA} ($r=4$) delivers $14.57 \pm 0.07$ dB (+4.06 dB over zero-shot) and optimal perceptual quality (SSIM 0.369). Restricting parameter trajectories to low-rank subspaces or bias manifolds regularizes optimization against ray-level overfitting, mitigating the floaters and collapse that plague unconstrained sparse-view fine-tuning.

\subsection{Computational Trade-offs}
From a systemic viability perspective, a clear dichotomy emerges between inference latency and training overhead. While generalizable models bypass extensive optimization (requiring only seconds for ZS inference or $\sim$7 minutes for FT), their volumetric feature aggregation mechanisms result in catastrophic inference bottlenecks, yielding rendering speeds below 0.02 FPS. 

Conversely, the explicit multiscale hash representations of the per-scene models proved vastly more efficient during runtime. Despite requiring lengthier convergence periods (from $\sim$9 minutes for Nerfacto up to $\sim$32 minutes for Instant-NGP), they achieve inference rates ranging from $13\times$ (Instant-NGP vs. GNT) to $97\times$ (Nerfacto vs. PixelNeRF) faster than the generalizable baselines. Furthermore, the hash-based models exhibit a highly optimized dynamic memory footprint. While Instant-NGP operates at just 1.10 GB of VRAM during inference on the fern scene (Table \ref{tab:fern_mem}), the global average footprint across all scenes scales slightly to 1.43 GB for Nerfacto (Table \ref{tab:super_results}) due to structural complexity variance between captures. Nonetheless, these metrics solidify per-scene methods as the most viable path for hardware-constrained deployments despite the initial training cost.

\subsection{Regime Trade-off Synthesis}
The architectural trade-offs evaluated establish a rigid Pareto frontier between data availability and computational overhead, as visually summarized by the multidimensional radar chart in Figure \ref{fig:radar_chart}
. When multi-view data is abundant but inference latency and VRAM are strictly bounded, the per-scene paradigm (specifically Nerfacto) dominates the design space, delivering state-of-art fidelity with sub-second rendering. Conversely, under view scarcity ($N \le 10$), the structural priors of generalizable models become indispensable to prevent total geometric collapse, yet they exact a severe penalty: inference speeds plummet to impractical levels ($\le 0.02$ FPS) and visual quality plateaus prematurely, as the frozen feature extractor cannot adapt its priors to scene-specific geometric complexities (a manifestation of the representational bottleneck described in Section \ref{sec:fund}). Thus, the selection between regimes is not absolute, but entirely dictated by whether the deployment bottleneck lies in camera acquisition physics or rendering hardware capacity.

\subsection{Qualitative Analysis}

The qualitative assessment visually corroborates the quantitative metrics, specifically illustrating the boundaries of each architectural paradigm. For visual clarity, novel views matching the exact poses from the test set were synthesized using perspective projection.

Figure \ref{fig:comparacao_qualitativa} highlights the disparities in reconstruction quality on the highly detailed fern scene. As visually evident, neither PixelNeRF nor GNT achieved proper convergence. PixelNeRF produced renderings with intense isotropic blurring, a typical artifact indicating the network's failure to accurately localize 2D projected features across extremely sparse views. GNT, on the other hand, demonstrated a complete generalization failure regarding the in-the-wild characteristics of the scene. This resulted in a representational visual collapse, heavily distorting the volumetric geometry into noise, which persisted even after 10-view fine-tuning. 

Conversely, the per-scene architectures successfully captured the intricate foliage geometry. Nerfacto achieved the highest visual fidelity across all scenes (PSNR 27.11 dB, SSIM 0.875, LPIPS 0.171), recovering fine-grained details consistent with the ground truth resolution (Figure \ref{fig:comparacao_qualitativa}, bottom right).
