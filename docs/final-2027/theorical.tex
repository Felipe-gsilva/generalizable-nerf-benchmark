\section{Theoretical Background}
\label{sec:fund}

\subsection{Neural Radiance Fields and Volumetric Rendering}
Neural Radiance Fields (NeRFs) represent continuous 3D scene geometry and view-dependent appearance by mapping spatial coordinates $\mathbf{x} = (x, y, z)$ and viewing directions $\mathbf{d} = (\theta, \phi)$ to volume density $\sigma(\mathbf{x}) \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ and directional radiance $\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d}) \in [0, 1]^3$ using a Multilayer Perceptron (MLP) $F_\theta$ \cite{mildenhall2020nerfrepresentingscenesneural}. Sinusoidal positional encoding $\gamma(\cdot)$ maps the inputs into a higher-dimensional Fourier space $\mathbf{v} = \gamma(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{d})$ so that $F_\theta(\mathbf{v}) \mapsto (\sigma, \mathbf{c})$ represents high-frequency geometric and textural details. The color observed along a camera ray $\mathbf{r}(t) = \mathbf{o} + t\mathbf{d}$ within bounds $[t_n, t_f]$ is computed via the volumetric rendering integral \cite{mildenhall2020nerfrepresentingscenesneural}:
\begin{equation}
\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) = \int_{t_n}^{t_f} T(t)\,\sigma(\mathbf{r}(t))\,\mathbf{c}(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{d}) \, dt
\label{eq:vol_render}
\end{equation}
where $T(t) = \exp\!\left(-\int_{t_n}^t \sigma(\mathbf{r}(s)) \, ds\right)$ denotes the accumulated transmittance, which is numerically estimated through stratified quadrature sampling.

\subsection{Explicit Primitives, Hybrid Grids, and Generalization}
Evaluating coordinate-based MLPs across thousands of ray samples introduces high computational costs during training and rendering. Hybrid per-scene representations mitigate this cost by storing trainable features in spatial data structures. Instant-NGP \cite{muller2022} indexes feature vectors across multiresolution hash tables, reducing training times from hours to minutes. Nerfacto \cite{Tancik2023} combines hash encodings with proposal network sampling, spherical harmonics, and appearance embeddings.

In contrast to implicit ray-marching, 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) \cite{kerbl3Dgaussians} (implemented in Nerfstudio as Splatfacto) represents a scene explicitly as a set of anisotropic 3D Gaussian primitives $\{\mathcal{G}_i\}_{i=1}^M$. Each primitive is defined by a 3D center $\boldsymbol{\mu}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3$, an opacity value $\alpha_i \in [0,1]$, spherical harmonics coefficients $\mathbf{c}_i$, and a positive semi-definite 3D covariance matrix factorized into scaling $\mathbf{S}_i$ and rotation $\mathbf{R}_i$ matrices:
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i = \mathbf{R}_i \mathbf{S}_i \mathbf{S}_i^\top \mathbf{R}_i^\top, \quad \boldsymbol{\Sigma}'_i = (\mathbf{J}\mathbf{W}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_i\mathbf{W}^\top\mathbf{J}^\top)_{1:2,1:2}
\label{eq:3dgs_cov}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{W}$ is the viewing transformation and $\mathbf{J}$ is the affine Jacobian of the projective transformation. Given the 2D Gaussian response $\alpha'_i = \alpha_i \exp\!\left(-\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{p}-\boldsymbol{\mu}'_i)^\top {\boldsymbol{\Sigma}'_i}^{-1} (\mathbf{p}-\boldsymbol{\mu}'_i)\right)$ at pixel $\mathbf{p}$, a tile-based differentiable rasterizer sorts the primitives by depth and blends pixel colors from front to back:
\begin{equation}
\hat{C}(\mathbf{p}) = \sum_{i \in \mathcal{N}} \mathbf{c}_i \alpha'_i \prod_{j=1}^{i-1} (1 - \alpha'_j)
\label{eq:3dgs_render}
\end{equation}

Although per-scene optimization achieves high fidelity when training views are dense, sparse-view settings ($N \le 10$) leave the 3D reconstruction problem underconstrained, which often produces floating artifacts and inaccurate depth estimates \cite{niemeyer2022regnerf, jain2021dietnerf, seo2026improvingsparseview3dgsgeneralization}. Generalizable NeRFs address view scarcity by learning multi-view priors across large scene collections \cite{yu2021pixelnerfneuralradiancefields, chen2021mvsnerffastgeneralizableradiance, wang2021ibrnet, t2023attentionnerfneeds}. PixelNeRF \cite{yu2021pixelnerfneuralradiancefields} projects 3D query points onto source image planes and conditions a coordinate MLP on extracted ResNet features. The Generalizable NeRF Transformer (GNT) \cite{t2023attentionnerfneeds} replaces the convolutional backbone and MLP renderer with a Vision Transformer (ViT) encoder and an epipolar cross-attention decoder that aggregates multi-view features along epipolar lines.

\subsection{Fine-Tuning Strategies and Parameter-Efficient Regimes}
\label{sec:ft_theory}

When pre-trained generalizable models are applied directly in a zero-shot (ZS) regime to novel scenes, differences in camera intrinsics, lighting, and textures often cause spatial blurring under few input views ($N \le 10$) \cite{mildenhall2019locallightfieldfusion, yu2021pixelnerfneuralradiancefields}. Fine-tuning (FT) adapts the network weights to the target scene by minimizing the photometric reconstruction loss over sampled rays $\mathcal{R}$:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{photo}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}|} \sum_{\mathbf{r} \in \mathcal{R}} \|\hat{C}(\mathbf{r}) - C(\mathbf{r})\|_2^2
\label{eq:photo_loss}
\end{equation}
However, unconstrained optimization over few rays can overfit to the training views, degrading the pre-trained priors and introducing density floaters near the camera planes \cite{niemeyer2022regnerf, roessle2022dense}. Following the adaptation taxonomy proposed by Gupta et al.\ \cite{gupta2024beyondlora}, five fine-tuning strategies were defined for generalizable neural rendering:

\subsubsection{Full Fine-Tuning (Full-FT)}
All network parameters $\Theta = \{W, b\}$ across the 2D feature backbone and the 3D rendering network are updated simultaneously. Despite allowing full weight adjustment, Full-FT requires storing optimizer states for all layers and increases the risk of overfitting when few views are available \cite{niemeyer2022regnerf, gupta2024beyondlora}.

\subsubsection{Partial Fine-Tuning: Decoder (PFT-Decoder)}
Gradient updates are restricted to the volumetric rendering head (the epipolar cross-attention transformer in GNT or the radiance MLP in PixelNeRF), while the 2D spatial encoder remains frozen. Keeping the visual encoder fixed preserves the pre-trained 2D representations and directs adaptation toward multi-view radiance aggregation \cite{gupta2024beyondlora}.

\subsubsection{Partial Fine-Tuning: Backbone (PFT-Backbone)}
PFT-Backbone updates only the 2D feature extractor (the ResNet in PixelNeRF or the patch embedder in GNT) while keeping the volumetric rendering head frozen. This configuration adjusts low-level perceptual feature maps to the target textures and illumination without altering the pre-trained ray integration layers.

\subsubsection{Selective PEFT: BitFit}
BitFit freezes all weight matrices $W \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$ and optimizes only the additive bias vectors according to $y = W x + (b + \Delta b)$ \cite{zaken2022bitfit}. Updating less than $0.15\%$ of the total parameters acts as a structural constraint that shifts activation thresholds while preserving the pre-trained weights \cite{gupta2024beyondlora}.

\subsubsection{Additive PEFT: Low-Rank Adaptation (PEFT-LoRA)}
LoRA freezes the pre-trained weight matrices $W_0 \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times d_{\text{in}}}$ and represents weight updates through a low-rank decomposition \cite{hu2021lora}:
\begin{equation}
W' = W_0 + \Delta W = W_0 + \frac{\alpha}{r} B A
\label{eq:lora}
\end{equation}
where $B \in \mathbb{R}^{d_{\text{out}} \times r}$ (initialized to zero) and $A \in \mathbb{R}^{r \times d_{\text{in}}}$ (initialized from a Gaussian distribution) have rank $r \ll \min(d_{\text{in}}, d_{\text{out}})$, scaled by $\alpha$. Constraining the update to a low-rank subspace reduces optimizer memory and regularizes fine-tuning under limited supervision \cite{gupta2024beyondlora}.
